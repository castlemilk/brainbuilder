# Moderator script

**DRAFT · BB-RES-003 · 45-minute plan · v0.1**

Read the protocol and operations plan before use. Required participation/notes agreement must precede substantive questioning. Audio remains off unless separately authorized by the project and explicitly chosen by the participant.

Use the participant's language. One rich account is more useful than rushing through every probe. Do not praise answers that support Brainbuilder, diagnose a learning style or turn the session into product coaching.

## Before the participant arrives

- Confirm the current information/consent version, eligible primary cohort and approved format.
- Put the private contact record away; use only the study code in notes.
- Disable automatic recording, transcription, meeting summaries and screen capture.
- Confirm who is attending and make the participant aware of every observer.
- Have the withdrawal date/contact ready. Do not load a product demo into view yet.
- Check that the participant can join in a safe, stationary setting; offer a different time if not.

## 0–5 minutes: information and agreement

### M01 — Opening

“Thank you for speaking with me. We are trying to understand how people actually learn or practise things. We are interested in what works already as well as things that do not. There is no right answer, and this is not a test or a sales conversation.

“We will talk about one recent occasion first. You can skip any question, take a break or stop. Please avoid confidential work material or anyone else's personal details. You can describe something without showing it. Have you had a chance to read the information, and what would you like me to clarify?”

### M02 — Record separate choices

Resolve C01–C03 in file 04. Ask O01 and O02 separately, with No as an ordinary option.

“Is it okay for me to take the research notes we described?”

If optional audio was approved for the study: “Would you prefer notes only, or notes and audio recording to help check them? Either is fine.” Log the choice while audio is still off. Only then start an approved recorder if the person chose it. Confirm all participants/observers know it is active.

“Would you like any adjustment to the format or a break at any point?” Do not ask for the reason or a diagnosis.

If notes agreement is declined or unclear, do not continue to M03. If audio is declined, continue normally with notes.

## 5–20 minutes: the most recent eligible episode

### M03 — Choose the episode

“In the screener you mentioned [brief, nonidentifying topic]. Could you take me back to the most recent occasion when you tried to understand or practise that? Roughly when was it?”

If the screening example is no longer suitable: “Is there another specific occasion in the last eight weeks you can describe?” Record the change and apply the same cohort rule. Do not pick an episode simply because it fits the recruitment quota.

Do not ask the old opener, “When did you want to learn but only have a few minutes?” It assumes the very constraint this study should examine.

### M04 — Trigger and desired outcome

“What prompted you to do it then?”

“What were you hoping to understand, explain or do afterward?”

For overlapping jobs: “For this particular occasion, which reason most influenced your decision to do it then?”

Optional neutral cohort probes:

| Cohort | Probe |
| --- | --- |
| INT | “What kind of task were you preparing to handle? Please describe the task without naming the interviewer or organization.” |
| WORK | “What did the work situation require you to understand or explain? A generic description is enough.” |
| CUR | “What caught your interest, and what did you want from exploring it?” |

### M05 — Reconstruct actions

“What did you do first? What happened next?”

“What did you use, if anything? How did you choose it?”

“How far did you get, and where did you leave it?”

If useful and the participant is comfortable: “You can describe the material, or show a nonconfidential example if you prefer. Either is useful.” Ask permission before taking any artifact-derived note. Do not request a screenshot, account login, repository, receipt or document copy.

Record the sequence before interpreting friction. “I opened an app during this interview” is an observed interview action; “I used it every day last month” remains a self-report.

### M06 — Time, setting and attention

“Where were you, in broad terms, and what else was happening?”

“About how much time did you actually spend on that occasion?”

“Was that the amount of time you expected, or did something change it?”

“What ways of interacting were practical there—reading, listening, speaking, typing, doing something hands-on, or another way?”

Ask open questions before listing modes, and use the list only if clarification is needed. Do not assume a commute, phone, interruption or preference for short sessions.

### M07 — Stopping and returning

“What caused you to stop, if you stopped?”

“What happened the next time you came back to it, if you did?”

“How did you decide where to start again?”

If they did not return: “What happened instead?” A decision to rest or prioritize another task is not automatically a failure to learn.

### M08 — What came of it

“What happened afterward that helped you judge whether it had been useful?”

“Did you need to explain or use the idea later? If so, what happened?”

“What are you unsure you could do now?”

Do not administer an impromptu quiz or label a remembered outcome as checked learning. Note when the answer is reported confidence, reported application, an inspected artifact or something actually observed.

## 20–30 minutes: alternatives, contrast and exceptions

### M09 — Existing approaches

“What other ways have you used for a similar purpose?”

“Which approach works well enough that you would keep it? Can you describe a recent example?”

“Was there an approach you stopped using? What happened the last time you used it?”

“Can you think of a time when a different amount of time or a different setting worked better?”

Use concrete comparison rather than “Would you prefer our shorter lessons?” Longer sessions, a conversation, paper notes, an existing tool or no new tool are valid outcomes.

### M10 — Routine and the choice not to learn

“When does learning fit comfortably into your day, and when does it not?”

“Can you describe a recent time you chose something else instead?”

“What, if anything, prompts you to come back to learning?”

If streaks, reminders or goals arise naturally: “What did that do for you on a recent occasion?” Do not suggest that gamification is harmful or that every available minute should be productive.

If commute or audio comes up: “When would listening work, and when would it be unwelcome or impractical? Is speaking aloud a different question for you?” Listening preference is not permission to record or proof that interactive learning works while moving.

## 30–35 minutes: actual past spending or refusal

### M11 — Historical behavior, optional detail

“For this kind of learning, have you used anything that you, an employer or someone else paid for? Free approaches count too.”

If relevant: “What was the most recent purchase, renewal, cancellation or decision not to buy? What led to that decision?”

“What did you actually keep using afterward?”

Optional: “If you are comfortable saying, roughly what did it cost and in which currency? It is fine to skip the amount.” Do not request a receipt, payment account, income or exact employer. Record self-reported spending as self-report.

No price is suggested. Do not ask what they would pay for Brainbuilder at this stage.

## 35–42 minutes: optional late concept reaction

Only reach this section after M03–M11, or after the participant freely skips particular behavioral questions. If the recent episode and alternatives are still unclear or time is short, omit this section and record “not shown.”

### M12 — Neutral concept card

Read exactly once:

“One idea being considered is a mobile lesson about one concept, followed by an optional question and a place to return later. It is a draft format, and we have not established who it is useful for.”

Then ask:

- “In your own words, what do you think that would involve?”
- “Does any occasion you described seem relevant to it? It is fine if none does.”
- “What might it replace, and what would you keep using instead?”
- “Where would it be a poor fit?”
- “What would you need to know before deciding whether to try it?”

Do not add AI, promised retention, an ideal duration, adaptive mastery, offline reliability or a guarantee about work/interview results. Do not answer objections with an improvised feature promise.

### M13 — Optional hypothetical purchase-decision question

Default: omit unless the approved protocol specifically needs it and there is time.

“If a format like this were offered as a paid service, how would you decide whether it was worth considering, if at all?”

This asks about a hypothetical decision process, not a price or commitment. Do not supply a price anchor, take a payment, offer a trial or infer conversion from an answer. Tag all answers HYP and separate them from M11's reported history. An actual price test belongs to the later commercial protocol.

## 42–45 minutes: close

### M14 — Summary and correction

“Let me check that I understood: [brief neutral summary of the episode and any useful alternative]. What did I get wrong or leave out?”

“Is there anything you would prefer me not to keep in the notes?”

Remind the participant of their study code, withdrawal-window end date and approved contact. Confirm only the recontact choice already explicitly made; a friendly conversation is not an opt-in.

“Thank you. There is no expectation that you buy, review or recommend anything.”

Use the approved thank-you terms. If the interview ended early or the participant withdrew, apply the pre-agreed policy without pressuring them to finish.

## Moderator repairs

| Risky wording or action | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| “You must struggle to find time?” | “How much time did you actually spend on that occasion?” |
| “Would bite-sized lessons help you remember?” | Ask what happened later; keep the draft concept for M12 |
| “Why did you fail to keep the habit?” | “What happened when you next considered doing it?” |
| “Is the commute a good learning opportunity?” | “When does learning fit, and when is another activity preferable?” |
| “Show me your work document so I can verify it.” | Invite a generic description; declining an artifact does not reduce eligibility |
| “Everyone likes the progress feature.” | Do not give other participants' answers or a desired answer |
| Recording because the participant said “the interview is fine” | Record separate audio consent before enabling a recorder |
| Treating enthusiastic “I would pay” as behavioral evidence | Tag HYP; retain actual purchase history separately |
| Filling a silence with feature suggestions | Pause, then ask “What happened next?” |

## Immediately afterward

Mark the actual sections covered, approximate duration, assistance/prompts, deviations, consent changes and recording status. Separate REP/OBS/ART/QUOTE/INF/HYP while the notes are fresh. Keep contradictions. Set actual retention/deletion dates and move records only to the approved restricted store.
