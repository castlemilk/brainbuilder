## Design Context

### Users
Brainbuilder serves learners across age levels who want intentional microlearning on any topic, book, or skill. They may be at a desk, on a bus, or between commitments, so the product must support quick sessions, deeper guided paths, and tactile practice without requiring a long setup flow.

### Brand Personality
The interface should feel sleek, curious, and energetic. It should make serious learning feel approachable through game-like feedback, but it should avoid becoming childish or static.

### Aesthetic Direction
Use a warm, tactile sketchbook direction with crisp controls, strong typography, and restrained teal, ember, and violet accents. The platform should feel more like an active learning instrument than a marketing page.

### Design Principles
- Make every visible control do real work.
- Keep mobile workflows thumb-friendly and commute-ready.
- Turn abstract topics into concrete feedback, quizzes, games, and progress.
- Prefer dynamic data and clear loading/error states over static mock panels.
- Preserve accessibility basics: visible focus, readable contrast, reduced-motion support, and no hidden critical functionality on mobile.
